Sheet 1

The Request Line

Transit diagram Covers · 04 Networking and Internet · 14 Scalability and Reliability

One click, one route, twenty-four stops. Two stations let the journey end early, and almost all performance work is the effort to end it at one of them.

The five that cause most real damage

Station 14, authorisation forgotten — change an id in a URL and get someone else’s data. Station 17, a cache key without a tenant. Station 21, a money handler that is not idempotent. Station 18, the N+1 query. Station 08, a health check so deep that one slow database removes every backend at once. Test these; do not reason about them.

Sheet 8

The Threat Map

Blast radius Covers · 09 Security

Not a wall, because there is no inside any more. Concentric rings measuring one thing: when prevention eventually fails somewhere, how far does that failure actually reach? The answer is architecture, and it is decided long before the incident.

A secret in version control is compromised, and deleting it later does nothing

It stays in the history, in every clone anyone made, and in every fork and build cache. The only correct response is to rotate the credential. Treat “we removed it in the next commit” as a red flag in any review.

Gazetteer — every note on this sheet

The two questions

Authentication · Authorization

Who are you, and may you. Getting the first right and forgetting the second is the classic failure.

Holding passwords

Password Hashing · Argon2 · bcrypt

Slowness is the feature. This one decision determines whether a leaked table is an incident or a catastrophe.

Not holding them at all

OAuth2 · OpenID Connect · JWT

Delegate identity where you can. And know whether your token can be revoked before you choose it.

Writing safe code

Secure Coding · OWASP Top 10

The same handful of flaws, every year, in every language. Learn the list once.

Keeping secrets

Secrets Management

Not in code, not in config, not in logs — and rotated after any exposure, not merely deleted.

The rest of security

Cloud Security · IAM · Security Checklist

On sheets 6 and 4. In the cloud, identity is the perimeter, so most security work happens there.

Sheet 9

Five Real Systems

Comparative plates Covers · 15 Real World System Design · 17 Personal Knowledge Base

Five systems, drawn on the same axis so the difference is visible. None of them is impressive because of what it built — each is instructive because of the one thing it identified as its bottleneck, and the single trade it made in response.

What not to take from this sheet

Thousands of microservices, your own CDN hardware, a bespoke in-memory geospatial service, a Dynamo-style store. What does transfer, at any size: no shared databases, a timeout and a fallback on every call, idempotency keys on anything that moves money, and a consistency decision made per dataset rather than for the whole system.
